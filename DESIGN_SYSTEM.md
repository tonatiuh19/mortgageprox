# Design system

Calm, premium, and blank until a brokerage drops its color on top.

## Mark

The tab icon is the house on a rounded blue square. `public/favicon.svg` is the sharp version. `public/favicon.ico` carries the same mark at 16, 32, 48, 64 and 256.

## Color

Light background is warm off-white (`#FAFAF9`, token `--background`). Dark background is ink (`#0B0F19`). One accent (`--primary`) carries buttons, highlights and the wizard selection. Status stays on `primary`, `muted` and `destructive`. Do not introduce one-off Tailwind palette classes for product UI.

## Type

Inter for the interface. Fraunces (`font-display`) for headlines and borrower-facing moments. Amounts and counts use tabular numbers.

## Surfaces

Cards use a 16px radius, a hairline border and `shadow-card`. The recommendation step uses `shadow-glow`.

## Sign in

Sign in is a full-height split, outside the marketing header. The form sits on the warm background: the mark, a display headline, the email, then six code boxes. The email step button says Continue. It is disabled while the code is sending or the sign-in is checking, and that label ends with an ellipsis. A new code waits 5 minutes. If the address belongs to an active account and the code was sent, a short line says to check spam. On a wide screen the other half is an ink panel with the office line and a sample brokerage card. Under 1024px that panel is hidden and the form fills the screen. It fits at 320px with no sideways scroll.

## Office home

The dashboard greets the person by first name. A loan officer or partner sees their own book. An owner or leader sees the whole office, including seats in use. Counts for clients, open loans, open tasks, and unread conversations are links. Under them, open loans are grouped by stage, tasks due in the next two days are listed, and the five newest open loans are listed. The page asks them to open a loan, add a client, or open the inbox. Owners can review billing, and a due start fee uses that same path. The first paint is a page-shaped skeleton. The content fades in, and that fade is skipped when the system asks for less motion.

## Profile, brand, and the public site

The signed-in name in the sidebar opens the staff profile: photo, name, phone, language, NMLS, bio, timezone, specializations, email and text alerts, and a public link. Brand keeps the studio, logo, and menu, and also stores the office NMLS, street, city, state, and ZIP. A switch there holds a pre-approval letter until that loan's open tasks are done. The letters page only explains the current switch.

The branded website is five pages: home, about, loan options, questions, and contact. The owner edits the words. Colors and the logo come from the studio. The public pages live at `/w/{slug}`. Home leads with the bankers, Apply, and Schedule, then the page text. The contact form creates a contact. The office application lives at `/apply/{slug}` and wears the office color and logo. Each person also has `/apply/{slug}/{name}`, and `/book` and `/analyze` use that same personal path. A personal link opens that person's card first. The office mark sits on the card, then the photo or initials, name, NMLS, a short note, phone, email, the office address, and any links saved on the profile. Contact rows share one icon column. The visitor enters an email in a full-width field and continues. The page keeps a header with the office and the person, and a footer with that office's terms and privacy. The email screen has no step rail. After the email, a five-step rail starts at the name and fills as they move. Sending the application disables the button and shows a short wait. Success is a check, the office line, and a way to start another. When the letter goes out, the success screen also names the inbox. That letter uses the office logo, color, and corners. A purchase and a refinance each get their own document list. The button is Open your portal and lands on sign-in with the same email filled in. A code is not in the letter. The person asks for one on that page. Sending the application creates their portal account, so the code works, and the letter counts as one email. If the office is out of emails, the account is still created and the staff alert still goes. Reminder flows stay off until the office turns one on. Officers do not keep a separate policy. The pages belong to the brokerage. Continuing saves a draft on the server and in this browser, and the page address keeps that draft, so a refresh or a closed tab on this browser returns to the same step. Pipeline share, the emailed link, and replace all use the personal link. A file from it is assigned to that person, and their name shows first. Replacing the link retires the previous one. The form walks identity, property, finances, employment, and review, and a draft survives a refresh. Review opens with the loan amount, the purpose, and the address, then lists every answer in the same four groups as the rail. An empty answer says it was not added. Each group can jump back to its step. The scheduler offers open slots from that person's weekly hours. On Pipeline, Open a loan is one dialog: share that link, or walk client, property, loan, work, and review. The board itself stays a board. Cards show the address, the date, and the banker. A letter, the MISMO export, and the loan packet stay locked while the office hold is on and a task is still open. A partner can read the letter and cannot turn it on. The calendar shows bookings, tasks due, and birthdays. The scheduler keeps buffer, notice, and time zone. Reports and the home show funded volume and how many files have a letter. A broadcast can start from a message template, and an email template named Application is the new-application alert. A booking emails the office. The borrower home shows real task progress, a profile, and a note when a task is finished.

A loan file shows amount, value, down payment, LTV, property, dates, banker, partner, and realtor. Only a loan officer, a leader, or the owner can move the stage. The letter shows amount, maximum, expiry, and whether it is active. A held letter stays off while tasks are open. The checklist, documents, and compliance sit on that same file. The MISMO download is a 3.4 file of the stored loan, borrower, job, and property. It does not invent credit, assets, or a product. The document packet downloads the stored fields. Clients import from a CSV template. Reports list lead sources with count and share. The sidebar chip shows the photo, name, role, and unread count. Profile alerts can copy a second inbox and turn each event on or off. Blocked numbers show call and text badges and who blocked them. The income calculator uses salary or hourly pay, overtime, commission, bonus, and monthly deductions. Salary alone stays labeled an estimate.

Clients, contacts, and team can export the current search as CSV and can save that search under a name. Pipeline and tasks stay boards. Each of those screens, and the rest of the office, uses a page-shaped skeleton on the first load and a short fade after. The fade is skipped when the system asks for less motion.

## Portal shell

Every brokerage screen opens with the same header: a brand-colored mark, a display title, an optional count, and a Live pill when the list is current. Search, refresh, filters, and the main action sit on the right and wrap under the title on a phone. The mark, the Live pill, the buttons, and the title face follow the office color, corners, and headline. Desktop: fixed left rail, 272px, navigation grouped by category (Home, Grow, Inbox, People, Files, Setup in the brokerage workspace; Home and Network in the command center).

Under 900px, the same shell as Groundwork: sticky top bar, off-canvas sheet opened from More, and a fixed 5-column tab bar (four destinations plus More). Page scroll stays vertical. Wide grids sit in `.table-scroll`.

## Pricing and wizard

Public pricing lists Starter, Growth and Scale with a 30-day trial that opens after a $299 start fee. That fee is credited to the first invoice if the brokerage continues. Custom is quoted and does not include a trial or a start fee. The style studio is included in every plan and does not change the recommendation. Choosing a custom domain recommends Scale, where that address is included. Every brokerage also lives at the next free `{slug}.mortgageprox.com`. The receipt asks for that host, so a name that is already taken is shown with a numeric suffix instead of a second copy of the same address. The wizard is six steps: brokerage, team size, modules, the look, services, recommendation. Call transcripts and the Twilio connection are offered only after conversations or phone is chosen. Email, broadcasts and a custom domain stay available on their own. A request that sends transcripts or Twilio without those parents is ignored. The look is its own step: a color picker, a hex field, six starting colors, three corner styles and three headlines, with a live portal preview. Fill this later keeps the starting colors. A note on that step says the full studio is open once the brokerage is inside. Settings loads the saved phone, sender and color, and can change any hex color. It can also upload a logo and a favicon. The logo replaces the wordmark in the workspace and the borrower portal. The favicon replaces the tab icon while those portals are open. The same studio is in brokerage settings. The workspace and the borrower portal wear the saved color, corners and headline. Core modules are included on every plan. Pre-approval letters are a Growth choice, shown under "Add only what you need," and are not part of Starter. While the catalog or the recommendation is still loading, the wizard and the pricing page show card skeletons with a soft light sweep. On a long wizard step, a floating ink cue with a light label points toward Continue until that button is on screen. The last step is a receipt with an ink header, the monthly price, what is due today, the address, and the card form under Pay the start fee. While the payment is confirming, the button is disabled and cycles through four short lines, each with an icon and an ellipsis. The card fields fade and cannot be edited. When it succeeds, the questions, the step list, and the answers are cleared. The page is only the confirmation: the office name, the address, and Enter your office. The browser is signed in. Paying the start fee opens the workspace. A quote has no card. If the card session cannot open, the receipt says why. When the email already has an office, that box is a short note and a sign-in button, instead of an empty pay form. When the workspace opens, confetti falls across the page. Billing still shows the fee if it is unpaid.

## Billing

Brokerage owners and leadership open Billing from Setup. The page shows the plan price, then the six included numbers for that plan, each with the extra price beside it. It also shows the $299 start fee when it is still due, and six meters: seats, open loans, emails, letters, text segments and calling minutes. A meter is a standing cap (seats, open loans) or a monthly cap that resets on the 1st. Zero means that channel is not on the plan. An empty cap on Scale means no ceiling. Custom says the amounts are set with the quote. Past the included amount, emails, letters and open loans can take a one-time pack: 500 emails for $15 until the 1st, 20 letters for $25 until the 1st, or 20 open loans for $49, which stay on the cap. Texts and calling minutes pause until the 1st and are not sold as packs. Each person above the included seats is $29 a month on the subscription, bought before the invite, and the seat stays billed until the owner removes it. Twilio credits are a separate Twilio bill. MortgageProx does not sell them. Modules lives next to Billing. A module already on the plan turns on at no charge, and an optional one can turn back off. The daily tools stay on. A module that only exists on a higher plan shows that plan and an upgrade. The owner pays. A loan officer sees the lock and cannot pay. During the trial the subscription price changes and the trial end stays, so the first invoice is the new price and the $299 credit still applies. After the trial, Stripe charges the price difference now. Dropping to a cheaper plan is not a button. Custom is a quote. Records stay when a module is off.

Reminder flows live in Setup. A new office gets six flows, one for each stage from New lead through Funded. They start off. The owner or leadership turns one on. On a wide screen the steps sit on a canvas. On a phone they stack, and the selected step opens beside them. A step can wait, send the brokerage's email, send a text, notify the loan officer, check purchase or refinance, wait for a reply, or stop. Email steps use a template from the library, or a one-off that can be saved there, with the same branded preview. When a loan enters the stage, the email sends. A text sends on the office line when Twilio is connected and the plan still has segments. Texts wait for 9:00 to 7:00 Central. A reply stops the flow. Leaving the stage stops it too. A loan officer can open the flow and see their own book. They cannot edit it.

The loan pipeline is a board on every plan. Each stage the office has is a column, including Funded. Archived loans stay off the board. A wide screen drags a card. A phone shows one stage at a time, with a move menu. The card stays in place until the office confirms. The confirm names the borrower, both stages, and whether the file becomes funded or opens again. It also says when the reminder for the old stage stops and the reminder for the new stage starts. Each column loads 20 loans from the server, with load more, so a long New lead list does not hide Clear to close. Search, purchase or refinance, and assignee filter before the columns load. Assignee is there for the owner, leadership, and the processor. A loan officer sees only their own book. Moving a funded loan back to an open stage checks the open-loan cap, and the card stays if the plan is full. Extra stages scroll inside the board, not the page.

The task board is included from Growth. Scale and Custom include it too. Starter sees the lock and a link to the module list, including on a direct visit to the board. Documents are their own module and stay on every plan. Each column loads 20 cards at a time, so a full To do list does not hide the other columns. A loan officer sees tasks assigned to them, unassigned tasks with no loan, and tasks on their own loans or on a loan nobody owns. The home card counts only that book, and it is hidden when the board is off. Turning the board off cancels unsent reminders. The daily job still runs flows and task emails apart, so one failure does not skip the other. Tasks are a board with four columns: To do, In progress, Waiting, and Done. On a wide screen a card drags between columns. On a phone one column shows at a time, with a move menu on the card. A card has an assignee from the staff, a priority, a due date and time, and an optional loan. The office searches titles on the server and pages 80 cards at a time. A loan officer sees tasks assigned to them, unassigned tasks, and tasks on their own loans. When a due time is set, the assignee gets one email in the brokerage brand. Urgent is scheduled an hour before and again at the time. High is 9:00 Central on the due day. Normal is 9:00 Central the day before. Low does not email. Marking the card done, clearing the time, or dropping the assignee cancels the unsent reminder. Flows and task reminders share one daily check at 9:00 Central, so a reminder can arrive up to a day after the clock time.

The card form is Stripe's Payment Element, inside our page, with the warm background, ink text, Inter, and the brokerage accent and corner radius. The future-charge line is off, so the Stripe account name does not appear. Link's save-my-information block is off. The pay button sits under a checked agreement for MortgageProx's terms and privacy, including when the wizard is opened on a brokerage address. On the pay form, the borrower form, and the borrower portal, those pages open in a dialog so the form stays mounted. Open page sends the full page to a new tab. The marketing footer still goes to the page. Those two pages are Markdown, edited in the command center. Each brokerage also has its own terms and privacy, copied in the brokerage's name when the workspace opens. The owner edits them in Brand, and the platform owner can edit the same pages on that brokerage. The editor is a Markdown toolbar with the source beside a live preview. Visitors on the brokerage host or custom domain, and borrowers in the portal, see the brokerage pages in the brokerage's name and color, not the MortgageProx marketing site. Borrower forms start with the same agreement checked. A suspended brokerage does not publish its pages. Emails use one layout. A centered wordmark or logo, a short greeting, a large headline, the message, one button, a details card, and a footer. MortgageProx's own emails (sign-in, the office is open, an invitation, a call transcript) use the ink and blue MortgageProx look, and the platform owner edits the words in the command center. A brokerage's emails use that brokerage's logo, color, corners and headline style. The owner edits them in Templates, and the platform owner can edit the same emails on the brokerage. Texts and notes stay plain. Four starters are copied when a workspace opens: welcome, document request, task assigned, and a payment receipt. While the payment confirms, the button is disabled and each line ends with an ellipsis. On success the wizard is gone and this browser is signed in. The next step is Enter your office. The office menu, starter emails, and reminder library are written together, so the wait is the payment and the subscription, not one database row at a time. The welcome email is sent while the start fee is credited and the trial subscription starts. Paying the start fee marks it paid, credits that amount on the Stripe customer, and starts the monthly subscription in trial until the brokerage trial ends. Fulfillment runs from the Stripe webhook, and again when Billing loads if the checkout session is already paid. Set `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` before launch. Do not turn on automatic tax.

The MCP connector is a Scale choice. Billing shows reads this month and the dollar amount, not a pause meter. A successful read is 3 cents, added to the next invoice. Owners and leadership create the key here. It is shown once. The endpoint is read only: pipeline, clients and tasks. Writes are not included.

Campaigns are a Scale and Custom choice, and they stay off until the office turns the module on. A campaign is a list the office keeps. People join a topic and can leave that topic. The same list can receive another note later. A broadcast is separate: one announcement, sent once, to people already on file. Scale and Custom include 800 campaign contacts. Each extra 100 is $9 a month on the subscription until the pack is removed. Sends are not charged again and do not spend the email allowance. The office adds clients, contacts, or realtors. Nothing is copied when the module is turned on. The screen shows the pack price before the list passes the included amount. Every office shares one marketing contact pool. It holds 1,000 contacts until a platform billing user raises the capacity. A quote can raise the included amount for that office. Opens, clicks, and unsubscribes land when `RESEND_WEBHOOK_SECRET` is set.

## Phone

The phone desk stores browser ringing, a cell to forward to, a voicemail greeting, brokerage numbers, and blocked numbers. An inbound call rings the browser, then the forwarding number, then voicemail. A blocked number never rings. When Twilio and an office number are connected, Call rings the forwarding number from that line and then connects the client. A text sends on that same line. A broadcast email sends now. A broadcast text sends on the same line, up to the first 500 people.

Call transcripts are a separate package choice. When it is on, the words of a call are saved on the conversation and emailed to the assigned person. Live speech-to-text waits on Twilio. A transcript can be saved by hand until then.

Email can connect a Microsoft 365 mailbox. The person must sign in as that same address. Mail.Read and Mail.Send are requested. Calendar access is not. Reading the Outlook inbox waits until the Microsoft app is configured.

## Email

Sign-in emails are short, from `SMTP_FROM`, and do not use an em dash. Vitest and `NOTIFY_SANDBOX=true` do not send them.

## Local test fill

On `npm run dev`, opened from localhost or a private LAN address, create forms show a dashed "Fill test data" button. It writes sample values and does not submit. The public application uses that button too, and the sample email is felix@disruptinglabs.com. Production builds and public hosts never render it.

## Public footer

The marketing footer follows the page. The main column is at least one viewport tall, so the footer stays below the first screen and you scroll to reach it.

## Mobile check

After UI changes, confirm at about 320px and 375px that `documentElement.scrollWidth` does not exceed `clientWidth`.
